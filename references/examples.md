# Contrasting explanation examples

All passages below are original synthetic fixtures, not quotations from published works. Consult these local examples as needed; they do not prescribe the length or structure of a whole-work guide.

## A cold narrative and a desolate situation

Passage: She smoothed the award letter and placed it before her father. He used it to steady his cup. She smiled, holding the table's edge, as though she had finally found her proper place.

Distortion: She longs for paternal love and learns self-acceptance through her smile. This begins her growth.

Improvement: She offers the letter hoping her achievement will be recognized; her father notices only its usefulness under the cup. The final sentence brings her place in the family into alignment with the paper's function. The irony is cold, while her continued accommodation makes the situation desolate. Nothing establishes a turn toward self-acceptance.

Why: the interpretation follows actions, object use, and the final sentence rather than automatically reading a smile as release.

## Tenderness need not conceal cruelty

Passage: He forgot her name but pushed the bowl without onions toward her. She did not remind him. They finished slowly; the soup was still warm.

Distortion: This apparent calm hides a family's control over women.

Improvement: Forgetting has not been cured, but a memory of her preference survives in an action. She accepts this limited, concrete recognition. The ending rests on warm soup and shared time, allowing tenderness to coexist with loss rather than canceling it.

Why: the explanation allows tenderness to stand without using a generic theory to invent concealed malice.

## Similar events, different telling

A: He fell. At last everyone could see inside that expensive hat: there was nothing there either.

B: He fell. The crowd stopped; a child picked up his hat and brushed dust from its brim.

A produces ridicule through expectation and a double meaning of emptiness. B attends to assistance. Saying that falling symbolizes adversity fails to explain the difference.

## Boundaries of psychological inference

Passage: She brought the letter to the door. Hearing laughter inside, she folded it again and slipped it into her sleeve.

Distortion: She understands that he never loved her and decides to leave forever.

Improvement: Laughter interrupts her intended action; putting away the letter at least shows a pause in expression. The reason for the laughter, the relationship, and her next choice require context. One restrained action cannot establish an entire romantic ending.

## Convert notes into explanation

An internal note may say that object use and personal value are juxtaposed, supporting cold irony. The delivered explanation should guide the reader through the scene, as in the first improvement, instead of exposing note fields.

## Development across scenes

### Synthetic story: The spare key

When her mother moved into residential care, Lin kept the spare key to her old flat. Every Saturday she watered the plants there and reported that everything was in order. Her mother always asked whether the blue cup was still beside the sink. Lin said yes.

In winter the landlord gave notice that the flat would be renovated. Lin packed the books and gave away the plants. She put the cup in her own kitchen, where it looked too small among her bowls. On Saturday she told her mother the flat was empty. Her mother asked about the cup. Lin began to explain the renovation, but her mother interrupted: bring it next time.

The following week they drank tea from a cup and a paper beaker. Her mother took the beaker. Lin put the spare key on the table. Neither picked it up before the tea went cold.

## Plausible but underdeveloped versus explained

Both versions below concern the synthetic story above. The thin version is not simply false; it reaches conclusions before showing enough of their development.

Thin: The blue cup symbolizes the bond between mother and daughter, while the spare key represents the inaccessible past. The ending combines warmth and loss.

Developed: At first the mother asks whether the cup remains beside the sink. The question ties it to a particular arrangement in the former home, while Lin's weekly maintenance tries to preserve that arrangement more broadly. Renovation separates the cup from the place: Lin can no longer keep the flat unchanged, but she can fulfill her mother's request to bring one object. In the final visit, the mother takes the paper beaker, leaving the familiar cup for Lin. Whatever her unstated motive, the cup now participates in something they do together instead of merely certifying that an empty home remains in order. The key receives no comparable use. It lies between them without restoring access to the former life. Warmth and loss arise from these different trajectories, rather than from objects carrying fixed symbolic definitions.

Why the expansion helps: each return to the cup adds a changed circumstance or use. The explanation earns its conclusion through those changes; it does not repeat the claim that objects preserve connection.
