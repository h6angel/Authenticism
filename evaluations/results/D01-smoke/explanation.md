# The Yellow Wallpaper: when care becomes confinement

This translated illustration contains the ending. The historical smoke run read the English text and did not compare a published translation.

A woman's secret record unfolds during a period of rest arranged by her husband John, who is also a physician. Work and writing are restricted. She believes expression and company might help, but struggles to have her experience acknowledged. Being cared for and losing a voice happen together.

## A room takes over her world

Her attention increasingly settles on the wallpaper, moving from disgust at its design to a woman she perceives behind it. First-person narration brings us close without making every perception an objective fact. Her growing certainty makes the reading more disturbing.

## The ending's resistance and predicament

She tears the paper and believes she has escaped. John enters and faints; she continues creeping. Authority loses its composure, yet ordinary freedom has not been restored. Resistance and psychological crisis remain entangled.

The artistic force lies in a relationship becoming spatial and perceptual horror. Wallpaper develops through the telling rather than serving as a pasted-on theme. The story asks how someone can express suffering when others continually define and deny it.
